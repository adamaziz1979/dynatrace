1. Executive view

Conversion before customer-base expansion

Paid Insights is growing, but the Self-Service prospect pool is contracting. The short-term priority is to increase Business Insights penetration across the customer base Dynatrace already has before materially broadening the addressable base.

Replicate ANZ capability

ANZ combines local leadership, the deepest delivery core, strongest paid growth and the healthiest major-market prospect trend. Copy the operating pattern, not simply the staff count.

Japan as a distinct future-growth market; UK as benchmark

Japan has a small paid base but a relatively resilient Self-Service signal and requires a market-specific approach. The UK remains a maturity benchmark for strong customer-journey and business-value adoption.

Protect ANZ → convert more of the existing customer / Self-Service base → add India and ASEAN market leadership → share existing technical and analytical capability cross-regionally while demand is assessed → scale specialist capacity where evidence justifies it → rebuild the Self-Service funnel for future growth.

Key concepts: DEM and RUM

DEM · Digital Experience Monitoring

Simple definition: monitoring how users experience a website, application or digital service.

Purpose: connect technical performance to customer experience so teams can see where slowdowns, errors or poor journeys are affecting users and business outcomes.

Typical use cases: page/app performance, mobile experience, conversion and abandonment, digital journey health, geographic/device differences, and synthetic monitoring of critical transactions.

RUM · Real User Monitoring

Simple definition: a core part of DEM that captures the experience of actual users as they interact with a website or application.

Purpose: show what real customers are experiencing in production, including where performance or errors affect specific sessions, journeys and outcomes.

Typical use cases: page load time, JavaScript errors, crashes, user sessions, clicks, device/browser performance, transaction completion, conversion and abandonment.

Relationship: RUM provides the real-user evidence. DEM brings together the broader digital-experience picture. Business Insights then asks: what does that mean for the business, what should the customer do about it, and what value did the action create?

RUM → what users experienced
DEM → how healthy the digital experience is
Business Insights → what it means commercially and what action to take

Team

  • ANZ: 5 people: Manager, Sr Analyst, 2× Sr Support, Analyst.
  • Singapore: 3 people: 2× Sr Analyst + Analyst; no local lead.
  • India: 2 people: Lead Support + Sr Analyst.

  • Insights paid customers: ~+10% QoQ, +19% YoY overall.
  • ANZ: ~+22% QoQ and more than doubling YoY.
  • Self-service/prospects: ~-13% QoQ, -8% YoY overall.
  • ASK in the case (treated in this report as ASEAN + South Korea): the clearest funnel risk. Singapore is the current resource base for this grouping; India is also contracting.
  • Japan + UKI: flat/better self-service, but Japan's base is tiny and percentage movement is noisy.

The case explicitly identifies uneven maturity, time-zone/cultural complexity, distributed-team leadership, limited pre-sales capacity and inconsistent articulation of digital-experience value. That makes the problem an operating-model + conversion challenge, not only a staffing challenge.

3. Three-tier prospect model

T1

Strong Insights prospect

High digital journey exposure, RUM/DEM relevance, measurable business KPIs and enough scale/complexity for specialist analysis to add incremental value.

T2

Strategic nurture / pilot

Important account with mature Dynatrace adoption, but public evidence is mainly operational. Qualify a business-outcome use case first.

T3

Monitor / validate trigger

No clear incremental Insights case from public evidence. If the account is currently Self-Service, maintain that motion unless triggers emerge such as growing RUM, new digital channels, conversion issues or executive KPI demand.

Tiers indicate prospect motion, not customer value. Public evidence cannot reliably establish whether a named customer already buys Business Insights.

2. Delivery depth

Five of the ten APAC resources are in ANZ, including senior support and analytics roles.

7. Three-stage growth strategy

Short term · convert

Maximise value from the customer base already in front of Dynatrace

Objective: increase Business Insights penetration across the existing customer and Self-Service base before materially expanding the customer base.

  • ANZ: protect the strongest market and codify its sales, customer-engagement and delivery practices into the repeatable APAC conversion template.
  • ASEAN: use Singapore's existing 3-person analyst base more effectively and add an ASEAN Manager in Singapore to strengthen presales, customer relationships, sales enablement and Business Insights conversion. South Korea remains covered from Singapore initially.
  • India: add a market-building Manager / Market Lead focused on executive relationships, local sales support and conversion of the concentrated current base.
  • Account selection: prioritise high-fit customers where DEM/RUM evidence can be translated into measurable commercial or service outcomes.

Principle: do not make broad customer-base expansion the first growth lever. First increase the value and Business Insights penetration of customers Dynatrace already has.

Mid term · scale capability

Let demonstrated demand determine the delivery footprint

Objective: after the India and ASEAN leadership roles are established, use real conversion, workload and pipeline evidence to determine the right mix of technical and analytical capacity.

  • Assess the constraint: is growth limited by analyst capacity, technical support, senior expertise, presales coverage or local-market capability?
  • Scale by role, not just headcount: add Analysts / Senior Analysts where Business Insights delivery becomes the bottleneck, and Support / Senior Support where technical engagement becomes the constraint.
  • Use shared capability first: continue using existing Analyst, Senior Analyst, Support and Senior Support resources across regions while the new managers establish the actual demand pattern.
  • Localise only where needed: add dedicated capacity in a region only when conversion, utilisation, support workload, account complexity or qualified pipeline shows a sustained constraint.
  • Decision discipline: avoid pre-allocating delivery headcount before the new market leaders expose the real demand pattern.

Principle: leadership first → conversion → workload evidence → targeted technical / analytical scaling.

Long term · replenish

Rebuild the Self-Service funnel that feeds future growth

Objective: reverse the decline in Self-Service prospects, increase the pool of eligible accounts and create a healthier, more diversified pipeline for future paid Business Insights growth.

  • Funnel growth: make Self-Service account growth, qualification and progression core commercial measures.
  • Market expansion: broaden the addressable customer base only once the conversion model and delivery model are working reliably.
  • Japan: treat as a distinct strategic market. Its paid base is too small to establish a reliable growth trend, but the Self-Service funnel has held flat or better while most APAC markets have declined.
  • Capability: add dedicated local-market, language and delivery resources only when the funnel and paid conversion engine justify sustained investment.

Principle: grow the funnel, diversify the opportunity base, then scale behind proven demand.

Recommended sequencing

StageHorizonPrimary actionDecision gate
1 · Convert 0–12 months Build a named conversion list, codify the ANZ playbook, establish an India Market Lead and ASEAN Manager in Singapore, keep South Korea under Singapore coverage initially, and maximise Business Insights penetration across the existing customer / Self-Service base. Conversion improves, executive access increases and the current base produces measurable Business Insights growth.

3 · Replenish 12–36+ months Grow the Self-Service funnel, broaden the addressable account base and build market-specific capability in regions such as Japan where demand becomes repeatable. Self-Service growth, paid conversion and account density support sustained regional expansion.

Self-Service account growth, qualified pipeline coverage, progression rate and number of Tier-1 prospects entering the funnel.

8. Other Ideas

Stage 2 execution option · use existing technical and analyst capability cross-regionally while new managers assess demand

Idea: this operationalises the mid-term strategy. Before adding new technical-support or analyst capacity in each market, use the existing APAC resource pool more flexibly across regions while the new India and ASEAN Managers establish customer relationships, improve conversion and identify where the real delivery constraints sit.

Why it could work
APAC already has a mix of Manager, Analyst, Senior Analyst, Support and Senior Support capability across ANZ, Singapore and India. Shared cross-regional support can absorb early demand, avoid premature hiring and give the new managers time to determine whether each market needs more analytics capacity, deeper technical support, senior expertise or local-language capability.
What to watch
Cross-regional coverage should be temporary and capacity-managed. Time zones, cultural context, customer continuity and local-market knowledge can become constraints if shared resources are stretched too far.

Decision gate: after the new managers have established a clearer view of pipeline, conversion, utilisation, support demand and account complexity, decide where to add dedicated Analyst, Senior Analyst, Support or Senior Support capacity.

Interim operating model: share specialist technical and analytical capability across APAC first; localise additional capacity only when sustained demand shows exactly what each region needs.

Regional leadership decision matrix · culture, language and timezone

Purpose: compare the structural fit of different leadership scopes before committing to additional management headcount. Scores are whole numbers only on a 1–5 scale, where 5 = strongest fit. The matrix is a planning aid, not an assessment of any current Dynatrace individual.

Scoring logic
  • Cultural / business fit · 40% — ability to operate effectively with local relationship norms, decision styles and business etiquette.
  • Language fit · 35% — practical ability to engage enterprise customers and partners in English without creating avoidable communication risk.
  • Timezone overlap · 25% — workable business-day overlap from the proposed manager base.
Weighted fit is shown as a whole-number score out of 100: ((Culture × 40) + (Language × 35) + (Timezone × 25)) ÷ 5. Culture and language are weighted more heavily because timezone inconvenience can usually be managed more easily than weak local communication or relationship fit.

What the matrix suggests for two- and three-manager structures

2 Managers · timezone-led model

Sydney: ANZ + Japan + South Korea
Singapore: ASEAN + India

Strength: strong working-hour alignment across both leadership zones.

Constraint: the Sydney leader still carries meaningful Japanese/Korean language and cultural risk.

2 Managers · culture/language-led model

Sydney: ANZ + India + ASEAN
North Asia: Japan + South Korea

Strength: gives the highest-risk North Asian markets dedicated market leadership immediately.

Constraint: Sydney-to-India timezone overlap and the breadth of the ANZ + India + ASEAN span create a heavier management load.

3 Managers · mature-state model

Sydney: ANZ
Singapore: ASEAN + India
North Asia: Japan + South Korea

Strength: strongest combined alignment across culture, language and working hours, with clear regional accountability.

Constraint: requires two incremental Managers rather than one.

Interpretation: both two-manager structures involve a deliberate compromise. If Dynatrace treats cultural and language barriers as harder constraints than timezone, the culture/language-led two-manager model becomes more defensible despite the weaker Sydney–India overlap. The three-manager structure is the cleanest mature-state model, but should only be adopted when customer density, conversion and manager workload justify the additional leadership cost.

Decision rule: start with the leanest model that preserves customer effectiveness. Use local-language/customer access, presales conversion, manager workload and team support quality as the triggers for splitting a broad region into dedicated leadership.

Leadership design principle: use timezone to shape workable regions, but let cultural and language barriers determine when a market needs dedicated leadership.

APAC leadership hierarchy option

If the leadership structure grows to three Managers, the current ANZ Manager could also evolve into an APAC lead with the Singapore and North Asia Managers reporting into that role. This separates regional customer ownership from overall APAC people leadership and operating consistency.

Potential North Asia leadership model · Japan + South Korea

Idea: as the Japan and South Korea opportunity develops, consider a dedicated North Asia Manager covering both markets rather than permanently managing South Korea from Singapore.

Why it could work
Japan and South Korea have distinct market, language and relationship requirements. A leader with credible experience across both markets could provide closer North Asia ownership than a Singapore-based ASEAN structure while keeping Japan separate from Southeast Asia.
Why it should be conditional
The case supports cultural and language differences as real operating constraints, but it does not prove that a combined Japan + South Korea role is the right final structure. The talent profile may also be difficult to find.

Decision gate: under the base strategy, create this role when Japan + South Korea show enough combined Self-Service pipeline, paid conversion opportunity and account density to justify dedicated leadership. Under the culture/language-led two-manager option, this role would instead be the single short-term management hire. The matrix above makes that trade-off explicit.

Operating-model option: North Asia can either remain an evidence-triggered future role or become the first incremental Manager if Dynatrace chooses to prioritise cultural/language fit over the Sydney–India timezone trade-off.
