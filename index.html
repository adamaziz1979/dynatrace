1. Executive view

Demand before headcount

Paid Insights is growing, but the self-service/prospect pool is contracting. The priority is better conversion of the right installed-base accounts.

Replicate ANZ capability

ANZ combines local leadership, the deepest delivery core, strongest paid growth and the healthiest major-market prospect trend. Copy the operating pattern, not simply the staff count.

Japan as a pilot; UK as benchmark

Japan contains high-value digital accounts but a small base. UK provides a mature comparison for what strong customer-journey and business-value adoption looks like.

Protect ANZ → identify high-value self-service accounts → convert on measurable business outcomes → strengthen regional ownership → scale headcount only where pipeline and utilisation justify it.

Key concepts: DEM and RUM

DEM · Digital Experience Monitoring

Simple definition: monitoring how users experience a website, application or digital service.

Purpose: connect technical performance to customer experience so teams can see where slowdowns, errors or poor journeys are affecting users and business outcomes.

Typical use cases: page/app performance, mobile experience, conversion and abandonment, digital journey health, geographic/device differences, and synthetic monitoring of critical transactions.

RUM · Real User Monitoring

Simple definition: a core part of DEM that captures the experience of actual users as they interact with a website or application.

Purpose: show what real customers are experiencing in production, including where performance or errors affect specific sessions, journeys and outcomes.

Typical use cases: page load time, JavaScript errors, crashes, user sessions, clicks, device/browser performance, transaction completion, conversion and abandonment.

Relationship: RUM provides the real-user evidence. DEM brings together the broader digital-experience picture. Business Insights then asks: what does that mean for the business, what should the customer do about it, and what value did the action create?

RUM → what users experienced
DEM → how healthy the digital experience is
Business Insights → what it means commercially and what action to take

2. Case-study facts

Team

  • ANZ: 5 people: Manager, Sr Analyst, 2× Sr Support, Analyst.
  • Singapore: 3 people: 2× Sr Analyst + Analyst; no local lead.
  • India: 2 people: Lead Support + Sr Analyst.

Commercial signal

  • Insights paid customers: ~+10% QoQ, +19% YoY overall.
  • ANZ: ~+22% QoQ and more than doubling YoY.
  • Self-service/prospects: ~-13% QoQ, -8% YoY overall.
  • ASK: largest funnel risk; India also contracting.
  • Japan + UKI: flat/better self-service, but Japan's base is tiny and percentage movement is noisy.

The case explicitly identifies uneven maturity, time-zone/cultural complexity, distributed-team leadership, limited pre-sales capacity and inconsistent articulation of digital-experience value. That makes the problem an operating-model + conversion challenge, not only a staffing challenge.

3. Three-tier prospect model

T1

Strong Insights prospect

High digital journey exposure, RUM/DEM relevance, measurable business KPIs and enough scale/complexity for specialist analysis to add incremental value.

T2

Strategic nurture / pilot

Important account with mature Dynatrace adoption, but public evidence is mainly operational. Qualify a business-outcome use case first.

T3

Keep self-service / monitor

No clear incremental Insights case from public evidence. Revisit on triggers such as growing RUM, new digital channels, conversion issues or executive KPI demand.

Tiers indicate prospect motion, not customer value. Public evidence cannot reliably establish whether a named customer already buys Business Insights.

5. Why ANZ is the best internal success template

1. Leadership density

The only on-ground manager sits in ANZ, supporting coaching, customer relationships and local sales enablement.

2. Delivery depth

Five of the ten APAC resources are in ANZ, including senior support and analytics roles.

3. Market/customer fit

The public base includes banks, wagering, ticketing, utilities and digital government services where experience can be tied to revenue, transaction success or service outcomes.

Public customer archetypes

  • Village Roadshow: strongest direct commercial example: roughly $5m revenue opportunity protected.
  • Macquarie / BNZ: millions of banking customers and critical digital journeys.
  • RWWA: high-value digital channels with customer engagement and revenue exposure.
  • Horizon Power / IP Australia: prove that Insights can also quantify service reliability, cost and citizen outcomes.
Causality caution: the deck proves aggregate ANZ paid-Insights growth, but public sources do not identify which named ANZ customers are paid Business Insights customers. Treat this customer mix as an archetype explanation, not contract attribution.

ANZ template: Platform adoption → DEM/RUM → measurable business KPI → analyst-led insight → action → verified outcome → customer story → sales enablement → next conversion.

6. Testing your three ideas

1. Add market-building leadership in India and ASEAN/ASK?

India: add a Business Insights Manager / Market Lead. The role should be designed primarily to develop the market, strengthen customer relationships, support presales, identify self-service → paid Insights opportunities and build the local team over time, rather than simply supervise two existing resources.

ASEAN/ASK: add a Regional Manager based in Singapore. Singapore already has two Senior Analysts and one Analyst but no local manager, making it the most logical ASEAN hub. The manager should own Singapore/ASEAN and initially South Korea, with explicit conversion, customer-development and team-leadership accountability.

2. Japan as a high-value, distinct market?

Yes, and keep Japan organisationally distinct. Globeride and DeNA provide strong digital/RUM-led archetypes, but Japan should not be folded into the ASEAN operating model. Language, enterprise buying patterns, relationship-building and business etiquette justify a dedicated market plan. Start with targeted account development and Japanese-speaking senior coverage, then add a Japan Manager when pipeline and paid-Insights conversion justify a full local leadership role.

3. UK as the comparison market?

Yes. The UK is useful as a maturity benchmark, not an APJC target. BT, Next, Vitality, Nationwide, TSB and Mitchells & Butlers demonstrate the account characteristics APJC should look for: large digital populations, RUM/customer journeys, business KPI linkage and measurable financial/service outcomes.

Recommendation 1
Add an India Market Lead and an ASEAN/ASK Manager based in Singapore, with commercial conversion and customer-development accountability.
Recommendation 2
Keep Japan distinct; run a targeted high-value account program and build Japanese-speaking leadership as the pipeline matures.
Recommendation 3
Reverse-engineer the UK maturity playbook and apply its conversion triggers to ANZ, India, ASK and Japan.

7. Short-term and long-term plan

Short term · FY27

Grow paid Insights faster than delivery headcount

Objective: improve APAC growth by converting more of the existing Dynatrace installed base into paid Business Insights customers, particularly in India and ASK, while holding analyst/delivery headcount broadly stable and making targeted leadership investments.

  • India: add a market-building Manager / Market Lead, then concentrate on the small existing account base and identify 3–5 high-value conversion candidates with strong customer-journey, RUM/DEM or executive KPI use cases. Shriram Capital and Bajaj Allianz-type accounts are the clearest public archetypes.
  • ASK: repair the funnel by targeting mature digital banks and payments customers such as CIMB, KBTG and PayMaya-type accounts where Dynatrace is already embedded and business outcomes can be quantified.
  • Singapore / ASEAN: establish a Regional Manager in Singapore to lead the existing analyst pool, support local sales and own ASEAN/ASK conversion and customer development.
  • ANZ: protect the strongest-performing region and codify its sales, customer-engagement and delivery practices into a repeatable conversion playbook for the rest of APAC.

FY27 principle: invest selectively in market leadership, but monetise the analyst capacity already in place before materially expanding delivery headcount.

Long term · beyond FY27

Scale only where account density and conversion prove the market

Objective: grow the number of addressable accounts and then add regional capability where the economics justify dedicated coverage.

  • India: increase account coverage and analyst capacity after conversion improves. India offers attractive long-term scale economics because senior delivery resources can be added at materially lower cost than ANZ or Singapore.
  • Japan: operate as a distinct market with its own account strategy. Start with existing digital-native and RUM-intensive customers, use Japanese-speaking senior coverage, and add a dedicated Japan Manager/team when a repeatable paid-Insights pipeline exists.
  • ASK: add local-language or market-specific resources only where a cluster of qualified accounts justifies it; avoid duplicating management structures across small markets.
  • Operating model: evolve toward an APAC model with ANZ, India, ASEAN/ASK and Japan as distinct commercial leadership domains, while sharing specialist analyst capacity where practical.

Long-term principle: first prove the conversion engine, then scale the customer base and delivery model behind it.

FY27 measures that should govern the plan

Conversion
Self-service / eligible account → paid Insights conversion rate, with India and ASK reported separately.
Growth efficiency
Paid Insights customer and revenue growth relative to analyst/delivery headcount growth. Targeted manager hires are treated as market-development investments.
Market quality
Qualified pipeline coverage, number of Tier-1 prospects, customer concentration and percentage of recommendations that lead to measurable action.
Important challenge to the short-term thesis: India and ASK should not be targeted simply because resources already exist there. The case shows India resting on a very small number of paid accounts and ASK carrying the clearest funnel weakness. The short-term opportunity is therefore focused conversion of high-fit existing customers, not a broad regional growth campaign. If conversion does not improve, preserving headcount alone will not lift FY27 growth.

Short term: extract more growth from the installed base without materially increasing cost.
Long term: expand the account base in India and Japan, then scale local capability only after the conversion model is proven.
